Factor 1 — P

The spot anchor is where valuations die quietly

P.1

Commercial grade

ICE Arabica C-price plus origin differential plus FX. Public, auditable, no argument.

P.2

Specialty grade

C-price under-prices a 90-point lot by a wide margin. Specialty needs a transaction index we build from our own fills, partner roaster purchases and auction results.

P.3

Three traps

Futures up while the differential collapses; USD-denominated collateral against a CNY loan; and closing prices that gap through a margin call overnight.

Factor 2 — Q

Quality must be asymmetric

Good quality caps at 1.15. Poor quality can fall to 0.65. An up-only curve invites sample beautification; the downside is what a lender actually holds.

The most common mistake is double counting. If the differential already reflects a specialty premium, Q must not add the cupping score a second time. Q should only capture the verifiable deviation from the lot's own grade — the part a third party can re-test.
Factor 3 — D(t)

Temperature is a financial parameter

Decay is modelled as conservative step bands, adjusted with Q10 = 2: every 10°C doubles the effective ageing rate. Cooling a vault from 28°C to 18°C is not an operating expense — it is a balance-sheet improvement.

Factor 4 — LTV

Nobody negotiates a pledge ratio

It is a residual: whatever is left after the worst plausible price move, the re-inspection discount, the cost of disposing of the collateral and a buffer for model error.
