Layer 3 · Compliance

Three locks.
All of them mandatory.

A valuation engine pointed at collateral that does not exist returns a very precise number about nothing. Compliance is not the cost of doing this business — it is the licence to do it at all.

LOCK 01 — TITLE

Registry number

A receipt is issued with a nationally unique registry number that records issuance, endorsement, pledge and cancellation. Its job is to make a second pledge on the same goods discoverable by a stranger.

Without it, duplicate pledge is not prevented — it is merely discouraged.

LOCK 02 — EXISTENCE

Independent audit

Three layers, not one: inbound quality inspection, unannounced stock counts, and an annual reconciliation of receipt balances to physical inventory. The inspection body must not be affiliated with the borrower.

The retained sample is the point. It is the only evidence that survives a dispute.

LOCK 03 — UNIQUENESS

Pledge state machine

A receipt holds exactly one state at any moment, and discharge is only executable on a lender instruction. Borrower cannot self-release; warehouse cannot release on its own initiative.

Two locks share one rule: no single party can move the goods.

Two registries, not one

The most common structural gap

Registering a receipt and registering a pledge are different acts with different legal effects. Operators routinely do the first and forget the second.

Operating rule: register, then disburse — never the reverse. The disbursement condition in the credit agreement should be a registry confirmation number, not a promise to register later.

Precedents

Two cases where the model was fine and the collateral was not

Both are worth remembering because the lesson is not about pricing. It is about whether the thing being priced exists, and whether it has already been promised to somebody else.

Case Control that failed Consequence
Shanghai steel trade, 2012 Duplicate pledges, forged receipts, mutual guarantees between borrowers Large-scale bank losses; steel-trade credit withdrawn across the board
Qingdao port, 2014 The same metal pledged to multiple Chinese and foreign banks behind forged warehouse receipts Foreign banks exited commodity finance; the trust discount lasted a decade

In both cases the haircut models worked exactly as designed — on collateral that did not exist or was already encumbered. This is why the three locks sit above the valuation engine in our architecture, not beside it.

Red lines

Five rules that do not get negotiated

  • 1
    Register before you disburse. The registry confirmation number is a disbursement condition, not a follow-up task.
  • 2
    No disposal channel, no pledge ratio. If we cannot actually sell the collateral in a default, the LTV is fiction. Contract terms plus a named buyer.
  • 3
    The borrower supplies no parameters. Prices come from a third-party index, quality from an independent lab, the model stays closed.
  • 4
    Every valuation is written down, immutably. An auditable daily snapshot is the only defence when a counterparty disputes a margin call.
  • 5
    Concentration caps are hard. One borrower, one origin, one vault — each capped. A single-tenant warehouse is not diversification.
Legal and regulatory note. Warehouse receipts are recognised as documents of title and as rights capable of being pledged under the applicable civil and commercial law, and transfer generally requires endorsement plus the custodian's signature or seal. The precise registration channels, priority rules and licensing requirements differ by jurisdiction — including between Hong Kong, China and the mainland — and change over time.
